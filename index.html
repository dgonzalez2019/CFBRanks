How the rankings are built

The rankings answer one question: who are the best teams? Each team's score is its power rating plus 0.5 × its résumé points, so how well a team plays sets the base and who it has beaten moves it up or down.

  1. Power ratings from every game at once. Each team gets a rating in points versus an average FBS team on a neutral field. All ratings are solved together, so a team's rating reflects how its opponents did against their own opponents. Margins are compressed past about three scores, and all FCS teams share one pooled rating.
  2. Home field is measured, not assumed. The model estimates the home edge from this season's results. Neutral-site games get none.
  3. Each game gets a difficulty. The page asks how often the benchmark team would win that exact game at that venue. A road game at a top-15 team might be 25%. A home game against a bottom-20 team might be 95%.
  4. Strength of record. Shown alongside the rankings as a résumé check. Wins above benchmark (WAB) is actual wins minus the benchmark's expected wins, and SOR ranks teams by it.
  5. Win and loss quality. A win is worth 1 minus the benchmark's win chance, so hard wins score high. A loss costs the benchmark's win chance, so losing a game a good team should win costs the most.
  6. Résumé points. Wins earn 4 (Q1), 2 (Q2), 1 (Q3) or 0.5 (Q4). Losses cost 85% of the mirror-image win: 0.43 (Q1), 0.85 (Q2), 1.7 (Q3) or 3.4 (Q4). Beating good teams matters most, and wins count slightly more than losses.
  7. Quadrants. Opponents are tiered by power rank, at any venue: Q1 is the top 25, Q2 is 26–50, Q3 is 51–80, and everything else, including FCS, is Q4. Power rank is used so one team's résumé never inflates its opponents' ranks, which would let a conference's teams prop each other up.

The Results only tab

A second, independent ranking that uses nothing but who won. It follows the Colley Matrix method: every team starts even, a win raises a team and a loss lowers it, and each result is weighed by how good the opponent is, where the opponent is judged the same way from its own results. All teams are solved together, so beating a team that beat good teams counts for more. Scores, margins and home field are ignored.

The Matchup tab

Pick two teams and where they play. The predicted margin comes from a rating fit to every game's point margin (capped at 35 so blowouts don't dominate), plus a measured home-field edge. A quarter of the margin comes from passing and rushing efficiency instead: each team's yards per pass attempt and per carry, on offense and defense, are adjusted for opponents (a pass defense is judged against what its opponents threw for against everyone else), then turned into points using how yards per play related to scoring this season. The predicted total comes from each team's points scored and allowed, adjusted for the opponents it faced, and the margin splits that total into a score. The win chance reflects how far real results have missed predictions this season, so even a 7-point favorite loses fairly often.

Positional matchups

The Matchup tab compares each offense with the other team's defense unit by unit: passing and rushing efficiency, sacks and QB pressure (offensive line vs. pass rush), tackles for loss, turnovers (ball security vs. takeaways) and third downs. Each rating is adjusted for opponents, the same way as the power ratings. Only passing and rushing feed the predicted score, because adding the rest didn't improve predictions on past seasons.

The Lines tab

Compares our predicted line with the betting market's (the median spread across sportsbooks from collegefootballdata.com) for this week's games, biggest difference first. The track record replays the season: each of our predictions is made only from games played before that week, then compared with the betting line and the final score. Betting markets are hard to beat, so the record is the honest test of whether our differences mean anything.

Updating the data

Upload a CSV with the columns week,home_team,away_team,home_points,away_points,neutral_site,home_fbs,away_fbs. Rows without scores are skipped. An uploaded file only changes your own view. The published rankings come from data/games.json, which the scheduled GitHub Action refreshes from collegefootballdata.com.
