How the rankings are built

  1. Power ratings from every game at once. Each team gets a rating in points versus an average FBS team on a neutral field. All ratings are solved together, so a team's rating reflects how its opponents did against their own opponents. Margins are compressed past about three scores, and all FCS teams share one pooled rating.
  2. Home field is measured, not assumed. The model estimates the home edge from this season's results. Neutral-site games get none.
  3. Each game gets a difficulty. The page asks how often the benchmark team would win that exact game at that venue. A road game at a top-15 team might be 25%. A home game against a bottom-20 team might be 95%.
  4. Strength of record. Wins above benchmark (WAB) is actual wins minus the benchmark's expected wins. Bench % is the chance the benchmark would do at least as well. Teams are ranked by WAB.
  5. Win and loss quality. A win is worth 1 minus the benchmark's win chance, so hard wins score high. A loss costs the benchmark's win chance, so losing a game a good team should win costs the most.
  6. Quadrants. Opponents are tiered by rank and venue. Q1 is home vs. top 15, neutral vs. top 25, or road vs. top 40. Q2 runs to 40, 55 and 75. Q3 runs to 80, 95 and 110. Everything else, including FCS, is Q4.

Updating the data

Upload a CSV with the columns week,home_team,away_team,home_points,away_points,neutral_site,home_fbs,away_fbs. Rows without scores are skipped. An uploaded file only changes your own view. The published rankings come from data/games.json, which the scheduled GitHub Action refreshes from collegefootballdata.com.
